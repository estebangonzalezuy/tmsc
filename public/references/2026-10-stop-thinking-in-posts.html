Why it's here

A working system, not a theory. It gives a weekly loop, a long-to-short repurposing rule and the actual prompts she uses. It also contains a small, honest data point: her growth stalled when she stopped writing in series.

What it is

A long Substack post published 22 Sep 2026, subtitled "The 5-step system that took me from 13k to 52k followers across Instagram and Substack". It is sponsored by Metricool, and several steps are built around that tool's MCP connector and its Flows feature, so read those parts with that in mind. The PDF was uploaded on 2026-10-03 and is not kept in this repo (see README).

The argument, step by step

  • The diagnosis. Smart people get stuck because "you keep working your ass off to build a personal brand instead of just building a system". Discipline lasts a month, but a system takes the chaos out.
  • Step 1: two-sided ideation. - The artist's way: each month, pick 3 books, 3 podcasts and 3 newsletters (long form only) and spend at least 30 minutes a day on them, writing down every idea. Good input gives good output. She shares a "research librarian" prompt for choosing sources, with at least three of them adjacent to the niche rather than inside it. - The scientist's way: let the data choose the topic. Use her last 90 days of top posts by reach and by saves/shares, posts where competitors did 3× or more above their own median, and YouTube and Reddit. From that, list 10–15 topics (not formats), 10 ideas and 3 possible series, each with an end state, working titles and a first piece to test. - The key finding is that her best growth came from a roughly ten-part series (the "Claude MBA"), where each piece moved the reader closer to an end state they wanted. "The moment that series finished, I went back to standalone pieces", and that is when her growth slowed.
  • Step 2: one long piece a week. "Write one newsletter, film one YouTube video, record one podcast… every week." Her structure: find the idea, change the belief, share the steps (3–7), provide the proof, drive one action. She doesn't use AI to write the long piece, only for research, pressure-testing outlines, editing and proofreading.
  • Step 3: short form comes from long form. Her rule is that high-performing short videos need a good hook and novel information, and long form is what produces the novel information. Each long piece becomes one carousel, one reel and 3–5 smaller pieces, so 4–7 short pieces from "one tree". A prompt reverse-engineers her top 5 carousels against her bottom 5 into a reusable skill (structure rules, voice rules, failure patterns and a worked example).
  • Step 4: viewers into fans. Every short piece links back to the long one. A comment keyword triggers a DM with the link, which moves people onto an email list she controls.
  • Step 5: a calendar with one theme per day. Monday is filming, Tuesday carousels, Thursday long-form writing, and so on. There is also a monthly review prompt: what moved, best 3 and worst 3, what to stop, and one change. "Do not congratulate me… If something looks like a win but is inside normal variance, say so."
  • Close. "Your work should feel boring and repetitive. That is the biggest sign you are doing things right."

Notes

  • The review prompts are well written regardless of the tool, especially "label borrowed best practice as such" and "where the data is thin, say so rather than filling the gap".
  • The sponsor shapes Steps 1, 4 and 5. The ideas underneath (series, long-to-short, a monthly review) do not depend on Metricool.
