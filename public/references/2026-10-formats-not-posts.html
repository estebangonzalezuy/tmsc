If not posts, then what? What should tMSC make on a repeating basis?

Analysis, 2026-10-03

  • How I View the Internet
  • Social media gets a lot easier when you stop thinking in posts

The question

Both pieces, published two days apart, say the same thing: the single post is the wrong unit. So what is the right unit for the club, and what would change if we used it?

The references

2026-10-how-i-view-the-internet: Zack Evans

  • The unit is the format: "a new surprise inside the same container." The test is whether you could make 1,000 episodes of it.
  • A format is worth having because of what the audience gets. They learn the rules, start looking forward to the next one, and stop judging each piece on its own.
  • Consistency means consistency of promise ("what am I signing up for?"), not posting every day.
  • Short form gets you discovered, long form builds the relationship, and a format builds the habit. The post is the front door, not the house.
  • The idea comes first, judged by whether people would care. Data is how you learn afterwards.

2026-10-stop-thinking-in-posts: Tayla Burrell

  • The unit is the series with an end state. Her roughly ten-part series grew her audience, and going back to standalone pieces is when growth stalled.
  • The engine is one long piece a week, which becomes 4–7 short pieces. Long form is where the novel information comes from.
  • Every short piece links back to the long one, and the long one leads to an email list she owns.
  • The data comes first in picking topics (her own outliers, competitors' outliers, Reddit), balanced by a reading habit.
  • A monthly review with blunt rules: no congratulations, name what to stop, and say when a "win" is just normal variance.

Patterns

  • Same thesis, from two sides. Zack argues from the audience (why people come back). Tayla argues from the maker (why she stops burning out). Both say that thinking post by post means waking up at zero every day.
  • Long form is the anchor. Both make long form the place where trust is built and short form the way people find it.
  • A promise you keep. Zack's "consistency of promise" and Tayla's "end state" are the same idea: the reader knows what they get by sticking around.
  • Build, measure, learn. "Create. Learn. Repeat." and the monthly review are the same loop.

Where they disagree: what comes first. Zack puts the premise first, because no amount of execution saves an idea nobody cares about. Tayla lets the numbers choose the topic. A small, specialised club has thin data, so Zack's order fits us better. Tayla's review is still worth taking for what happens after we publish.

Takeaways for tMSC

  1. Pick one format and write its premise in one sentence. The club already has the containers: the Clips wall ("one clip, how it moves"), the Stills ("one frame, why it works"), the Directory ("one resource, who it's for"). Choose one, check it passes the 1,000-episode test, and run it on a fixed rhythm. The container stays the same and the clip or frame is the surprise each time.
  2. Run the Fundamentals as a series with an end state. Grids → Typography → Composition → Motion basics already reads as "four steps to seeing like a motion designer". Each page is the long piece, and short pieces made in the Posts Studio link back to it.
  3. One long piece, then 4–7 short ones. The Substack or a Learn piece is the tree. A carousel of the whole idea, a reel of the whole idea, and 3–5 pieces on single points. This would be the brief for the Desk and the content cycle once it's working again.
  4. Write down the promise. One line: what does someone get from following tMSC? If we can't say it, that is the first problem to solve. (It belongs in content/site.json, edited through the Studio.)
  5. Make the monthly review blunt. When the content cycle's review job is rebuilt, take Tayla's rules: name what to stop, flag normal variance, and give one change with what we'd expect to see in 30 days.

Open questions

  • Which container is the strongest format: Clips, Stills or something new? Collect three or four formats from motion and design accounts that do this well (a recurring breakdown, a weekly teardown) to compare.
  • What is the email or owned-audience step for the club? Tayla's funnel ends in a list, and ours currently ends at the post.
