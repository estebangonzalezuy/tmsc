Almost everything that ends up moving on a screen is type, or is there to
frame type. So before the motion, the type has to work standing still. The
good news is that most of it is four numbers, and each one is something you
can feel directly.

## Sizes come from a scale

Picking type sizes one at a time is how you end up with eleven of them, all a
little different. Pick a base size and a ratio instead, and every other size
is the base multiplied by the ratio some number of times. The sizes relate to
each other because they were made from each other.

The ratios have the names of musical intervals because that is what they
are, in size instead of pitch. A small one (a major second, 1.125) gives you
many sizes close together, good for interfaces with a lot of levels. A big
one (the golden ratio, 1.618) skips the middle and gives you drama, good for
a poster with one thing to say.

:::figure type-scale base=16 ratio=1.25 steps=5 caption="One base, one ratio. Move the ratio and the whole family moves with it. Most pages need three or four of these sizes, not all of them."
:::

I suggest picking one ratio per project and refusing to add a size that is
not on it. The refusal is the useful part.

## Measure and leading are one decision

The measure is how many characters fit on a line. The leading is how far
the eye drops to find the next one. They are usually taught apart and they
should not be, because a long line needs more leading (the eye has further
to travel back and can lose the next line) and a short line needs less (or
the paragraph turns into a ladder).

Somewhere between 45 and 75 characters is comfortable for a paragraph. You
can go outside that on purpose. You should know when you are doing it.

:::figure type-measure measure=66 leading=1.5 caption="A real paragraph. Push the measure past 80 and watch where your eye lands on the way back; then add leading and feel it get easier."
:::

:::note
On a phone the measure is decided for you by the width, which is why a lot
of mobile type feels slightly cramped with the desktop leading. If the line
got shorter, the leading can get shorter too.
:::

## Hierarchy is one lever, maybe two

Hierarchy is the reader knowing what to read first without being told. You
have a handful of levers for it: size, weight, case, space, and the typeface
itself. The thing to notice is that any one of them, alone, is enough.

The figure has the same three pieces of text every time (a kicker, a title,
a paragraph) and lets you pull exactly one lever. Every one of them works.

:::figure type-hierarchy lever=size caption="Five ways to say what comes first. Each one, on its own, does the job. The beginner's tell is pulling all five at once."
:::

Real pages pull two, sometimes three. Size and weight. Case and space. When
you find yourself using all five on one heading, that heading is shouting,
and the reader will lower the volume by skipping it.

## What to do with this

:::do minutes=30
Take a paragraph of real text (an email is fine). Set it at 16px on a 1.25
scale, 66 characters wide, 1.5 leading. Then break one number at a time:
measure to 110, leading to 1.1, size to 12. Read it each time, out loud if
you can. Write one sentence about what each break did to the reading.
:::
